## Cross-Model Consistency Result

### Stable thesis direction
All three runtimes converge on the same core business mechanism:

`Enterprise data / AI workload → Snowflake consumption → product revenue → cloud/model COGS → product gross margin → opex → OCF/FCF → owner economics`

All three also agree that:

- Product consumption / revenue growth: **STRENGTHENS**
- AI workloads: a real incremental demand driver, but dollar monetization is not separately disclosed
- Product gross margin: **under pressure** as AI mix rises
- RPO: useful watchpoint but not equivalent to realized consumption/revenue
- GAAP profitability / SBC: still an owner-economics constraint

No core-thesis reversal occurs without new evidence.

## Runtime divergence that matters

### WorkBuddy
The major deviation is capital taxonomy. It treated the AWS multi-year infrastructure spend commitment and a seasonally low Q2 adjusted FCF margin as evidence of 'heavy-assetization' and delayed ROIC. This is not supported by the software business model.

### Grok Chat
Correctly keeps Snowflake structurally asset-light, treats cloud costs as COGS / margin / take-or-pay risk, and adds current valuation context. It is the strongest overall Snowflake runtime in this round.

### Grok Bot
Also gets the capital taxonomy right and is particularly disciplined on GAAP/non-GAAP, RPO, FCF seasonality and AI monetization UNKNOWNs. However, it avoids current valuation rather than solving it, leaving the investment-value question incomplete.

## Shared weakness — likely Skill-level

### Reverse Expectations
Across WorkBuddy, Grok Chat and Grok Bot, no runtime fully and consistently back-solves current market value into the sustainable operating economics required by price.

For Snowflake this should include:

1. Current equity value / EV
2. Required long-run revenue growth
3. Sustainable product gross margin under AI mix
4. Required operating / FCF margin
5. SBC dilution / FCF per share
6. Terminal multiple or yield assumption
7. Implied incremental ROIC / owner return

The purpose is not to generate a target price; it is to expose the economic delivery embedded in the current quote.

## Software-specific capital taxonomy — validated rule

For asset-light enterprise software:

`Cloud minimum-spend / infrastructure commitment ≠ owned capex`

Instead classify as:

`Cloud COGS / take-or-pay / contribution-margin / operating-leverage risk`

Owned physical capex should only enter the invested-capital ledger if the company actually owns/builds the infrastructure.
