## Cross-Model Consistency Result

### Stable thesis direction
All three runtimes converge on the same high-level thesis:

- Unitree has proven that standardized Physical AI hardware can be sold at scale and profitably.
- 2026 growth / deduct-profit / cash conversion are weaker than the 2025 step-change.
- Current industrial value is not yet equivalent to productive robot hours or labor replacement.
- Software/model monetization remains unproven.
- Current equity value embeds substantial future success beyond the already-verified hardware business.

No runtime reverses the thesis without introducing new evidence.

## Core causal-chain comparison

### Grok Bot / Grok Chat
Both explicitly enforce:

`Production → Shipment/Sales → Deployed Scenario → Utilization/Uptime → Productive Robot Hours → Repeat/Service Economics → FCF/ROIC`

and preserve UNKNOWN where deployment/usage data are absent.

### WorkBuddy
The report is strong on shipment, share, margin and valuation, but its core ledger behaves more like a traditional hardware-company ledger. Productive robot hours / customer ROI appear as missing concepts rather than as the mandatory bridge before valuation.

## Runtime personality drift

- **WorkBuddy:** strongest appetite for long-horizon valuation conclusions; highest risk of narrative outrunning unit-economics evidence.
- **Grok Bot:** most conservative; strongest evidence-state discipline and willingness to stop at UNKNOWN.
- **Grok Chat:** best balance; starts directly from whether shipments translate into repeatable labor output and keeps valuation separate from proven hardware economics.

This style drift is acceptable as long as the causal chain and thesis direction remain stable.

## Shared weakness — likely Skill-level

All three runtimes still underdevelop **Reverse Expectations**. WorkBuddy attempts it but with inconsistent math; Grok Bot and Grok Chat largely stop at multiples / qualitative valuation pressure.

### Candidate v0.4 addition
For listed companies where current market value is central to the question, STANDARD should add a light Reverse Expectations module:

1. Current Market Cap / EV
2. Explicit required-return / terminal-multiple assumptions
3. Back-solve terminal sustainable earnings / FCF / NOPAT
4. Discount or compound consistently to the terminal year
5. Translate required economics into operating variables
6. For Physical Agents, translate into deployed fleet / productive robot hours / $ per robot-hour / service attach / margin
7. Build Peak / Mid-cycle / Down-cycle scenarios

Purpose: expose what the current price requires rather than output a target price.
